Cadency navigation redesign: mapping

Cadency navigation: current state to proposed structure

Draft for the Nov 20 regroup. Every box on the current-state sitemap is mapped to a home in the proposed structure. Deeper screens (create, edit, audit trail) follow their parent unless the table says otherwise.

Where this comes from

  • The current navigation is the sitemap on the Discovery Board (“Cadency Sitemap - Future State” section, captured from UAT 22 and 23 between Sep 29 and Oct 12, 2026), with every page coloured by unique visitors over 90 days from Pendo.
  • What each screen does comes from the Cadency Product Guide (Success Center courses) and Brooke’s Feature List by Module.
  • Who needs what comes from the Cadency Dashboard Research report (final Nov 19) and the Cadency UX Assessment (Sep).
  • The goals come from the kanban card “Cadency Navigation and Shell rewrite Strategy” and the Nov 19 mapping session with Brooke and Jenny.
  • The landing page follows the Florence Release designs in the Dashboards Figma file (Nov 12), and Admin follows the User Management prototype (Users, Templates, Access) and Jenny’s three admin roles from her Nov 20 notes on the first mock-up.

What the evidence asks for

  • Organize by task, not by module or permission. The research found people land in the wrong module and move between views to assemble their work. Brooke’s proposal and the session both started from task buckets.
  • One personal starting point. The research’s first phase is a landing page with favorites, documents and helpful links and admin news, growing into a work dashboard with My Tasks. The Florence Release designs already draw it; the proposal makes it the Home tab. Today that job is split between the R2R Console, Close Home, the configurable Home and each module’s dashboard.
  • Managers see across the team on the landing page and the module dashboards; administrators set things up in Admin. The Accounting Manager asks where work needs attention and who can move it, and gets that from the landing page’s My Tasks and the dashboards it links to. The Financial Systems Administrator asks what must be set up or fixed, and gets one Admin tab split by who holds the rights: user admins, system admins and module admins. Today both live under “Management” in Certification and “Configuration” in Close.
  • One place for each shared thing. User management exists in four places, reports in six, calendars in three modules, permissions in three, periods in three. The duplicate stickies on the board and the kanban goal “centralize organizational configuration and admin” both point here. Reports becomes one tab with nothing designed inside it, because the reporting workstream is about to start.
  • Keep what works. Match’s two-grid SmartMatch, the Certification role lists, and the dense tables are strengths in the assessment. The redesign moves them, it does not replace them.
  • Journal Entry gets its own top-level area. It has no tab today and lives inside Certification’s side menu, yet its dashboard is in the top usage tier.

Proposed top level

Area What it is for Who it serves first
Home The landing page from the Florence Release designs: favorites, My Tasks, documents and helpful links, news. Replaces the R2R Console and the configurable Home. Everyone
Reconciliations Certification’s prepare, review and approve work, open items, bulk reconciliations, QA review, my accounts. Accountant
Matching Dashboard, transactions (search, SmartMatch, exceptions), import control, jobs, daily reconciliations, as tabs on the page rather than a side menu. Accountant
Journal Entries Dashboard, journal entry, recurring, import history, ERP open items, binders. Accountant
Close Close Home, action plans, queues, results, status views, binders. Shown only to customers who use Close. Accountant, Close manager
Compliance Controls, results, surveys, scoping, code of conduct, binders. Unchanged inside. Compliance users
Reports One tab that opens today’s Reports screen. The eight report entry points land here; what is inside is the reporting workstream’s call. Accounting Manager, Finance Leader, Auditor
Admin Users (users, templates, access), System (organization, calendars and periods, data and integrations, application, maintenance jobs, notifications, archive, notices and documents) and Modules (each module’s configuration, accounts, entities and tasks). User admins, system admins, module admins

Outside the top level:

  • Help panel. One entry point on every screen. Cadency Help and Beacon merge into the integrated help work; the chat no longer takes the user away from their screen.
  • User menu. Profile, preferences, favorites (today’s bookmarks), change password, sign out.
  • Removed from customer navigation. The Disclosure Management and Workday tabs, Close Collaboration (zero views), Close Legacy Consoles.

Admin is one tab split three ways because that is how the rights are granted: a user admin manages users and permissions, a system admin configures the site, a module admin configures their module. What a manager needs across the team sits on the landing page and the module dashboards, not in a Management tab.
